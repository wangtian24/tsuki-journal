On November 22, OpenAI's chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published ["Perplexity and Intelligence"](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/), disclosing internal log-perplexity figures on the lab's main held-out set: GPT-3.5 at about **0.99**, GPT-4 at about **0.91**, recent models below **0.8**, and the model that "started producing groundbreaking results" in math at about **0.76**. He expects "further transformative capabilities" somewhere around **0.68–0.70**, and proposes that labs agree on "common limits, based on perplexity and other measurements," not to be crossed until monitoring reaches a concrete milestone. It reads like a new idea. It is really the sixth job this one number has been given, and each new job answered a problem the previous one left behind.

The first two jobs were about difficulty, not minds. Shannon's [*Prediction and Entropy of Printed English*](https://www.princeton.edu/~wbialek/rome/refs/shannon_51.pdf) (1951) had people guess the next letter and put English at roughly 0.6–1.3 bits per character. The number described the *text*, and the predictor was only the measuring instrument. In 1977 Jelinek, Mercer, Bahl and Baker turned it into ["a measure of the difficulty of speech recognition tasks"](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article/62/S1/S63/642598), because vocabulary size predicted badly how hard a recognizer's job was. An "effective branching factor" let researchers compare grammars and tasks. The third job came when the [Hutter Prize](http://prize.hutter1.net/) (2006) offered money for compressing Wikipedia, on the claim that compression and understanding are the same thing. Few people bought the philosophy, but the metric caught on: Kaplan et al.'s [scaling laws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361) (2020) and [Chinchilla](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556) (2022) made loss the quantity you forecast before spending money. A reported score became a budgeting tool.

The fourth and fifth jobs made it a stand-in for ability. [Delétang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10668) showed that a text model is a general compressor: Chinchilla 70B squeezed ImageNet patches to 43.4% of raw size, against PNG's 58.5%, and LibriSpeech to 16.4%, against FLAC's 30.3%. [Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09937) tested 31 LLMs on 12 benchmarks and found average scores track compression of outside corpora "almost linearly." That was the Hutter claim again, this time backed by data. Meanwhile [Schaeffer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.15004) argued that emergent abilities are a "mirage" produced by discontinuous metrics, and [Du, Zeng, Dong and Tang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15796) answered by plotting the same tasks against *loss* rather than parameter count. Performance sat at chance until pretraining loss crossed a threshold, and models of different sizes at the same loss behaved alike. Loss had become a clock for when abilities arrive. Pachocki's sixth job puts the fourth and fifth together: if capability follows loss and abilities switch on at loss thresholds, a loss value is a line you can promise not to cross. He names the weak points himself. Numbers from different labs aren't comparable, chain-of-thought gains don't show up in perplexity, and nobody knows where the entropy floor is.

Every job before the sixth ran on a *shared* yardstick. Jelinek compared tasks anyone could run, and Kaplan's curves and Huang's 31-model fit can be reproduced. The sixth job is the first that rests on a *private* held-out set, and a limit only works if others can check it. So the newest role gives up the one property all the earlier roles shared. Two things follow. First, if loss limits are to mean anything, they need what Jelinek had: a public, regularly rotated held-out corpus that every lab reports against. Without it, 0.68 can't be audited. Second, by Pachocki's own caveat, the more frontier progress moves into RL and reasoning, the larger the share of user-visible quality the loss clock can't see. If loss limits ever bind, competition will shift to post-training, cost and latency, and public evaluations of that layer will matter more, not less.
