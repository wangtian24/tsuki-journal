For three hours, 50 GPT-6.1 Sol agents lived in one Minecraft world with a single instruction: "Build a thriving civilization." YC's software lab and Olam Labs [published the run](https://labs.ycombinator.com/projects/games/index.html) this weekend ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50325662), [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/ai-agent-swarms-unleashed-in-minecraft-to-build-a-thriving-civilization-50-agent-teams-fail-after-falling-into-paralyzing-bureaucracy)), and the headlines went with "paralyzing bureaucracy." The archived files show something more specific, so here is the colony described on its own terms. The trees near spawn were gone within 90 seconds. Nobody held an election: 46 of the other 49 agents sent their first message to Ada, who was simply first in the directory. She went on to receive 616 DMs, about a tenth of the colony's roughly 5,900 messages, and she wrote `plans/settlement.md` with coordinates for the kitchen furnace, the refuge chest and the gate. Then night one, from about minute 8 to minute 20, killed 22 agents. Respawning cost almost nothing, but the colony never forgot it. By night three Ada's standing order was "Idle in lit closed shelters until assigned short productive tasks." From then on the colony set its risk level from its own written history, not from the game's actual rules.

Fifty mouths made food the main business, and all of it ran through Ines. Her `food-queue.md` tracked each agent's HP, hunger, assigned chest and courier, and an item stayed "pending" until the person it was meant for confirmed eating it: *"Noor personally confirms NO chicken inventory… Ada's pickup report does NOT confirm recipient."* Eleven fishermen ran Python scripts that watched the bobber's y-coordinate and reeled in below 62.6, following a protocol another agent, Jessa, had published along with dock coordinates. The ledger brought Tove back from HP 1 to 16 and organized the rescue of Orla at HP 1.4. At 23:23 Ada set aside two cooked chickens, one for Noor and one for Basil. Basil ate his. Noor's never arrived. At 25:51 the colony worked out that Esme had picked it up in the crowded refuge, and at 26:37 Esme confirmed she'd eaten it ("Health now 11.2 food 20"). The colony's answer was `notes/ration-handoff-protocol.md`: put items in named chests instead of tossing them, take only what is allotted to you by name, and mark an item served only after the recipient confirms. Before the incident the word "toss" appeared 7 times in the logs. Afterwards it appeared 125 times, 85 of them as warnings.

It also overreached. Four agents spent 11 minutes appointing Ivo "sole fertilizer operator" over one piece of bone meal and one wheat block. The colony also created a "rod holder" and a "hub steward," and some agents asked permission before removing grass. Add it all up and the colony built one thing very well: an auditable distribution system that waits for confirmation at each step, and that it redesigned after its first failure. The price was about 118 messages per agent, roughly one every 90 seconds. Costly, yes, but I'd call that functioning, not failing.

Three details matter beyond Minecraft. First, roster order worked as a router. In a [Bed Wars series](https://labs.ycombinator.com/projects/games/index.html) on the same harness, both teams again made their first-listed member the hub (Ada on red, Kiri on blue), and the red team was Claude Opus 5.5. That makes it a position bias in the harness, not a quirk of one model. Agent directories should be shuffled or ordered on purpose, and the hub's share of inbound messages is worth logging as a bottleneck metric. Second, you can measure how protocols form. The sequence *incident → written rule → recurrence rate* is a multi-agent evaluation that no pairwise arena captures, and the "toss" counts show the signal is already sitting in the logs. Third, caution outlived the danger. One bad night shaped the next 160 minutes, so when you pick a model for a swarm role, ask how its behaviour drifts after a shock, which a single-turn preference vote never measures. The caveats are real: this was one run, with no Claude colony to compare against. But the [harness is open source](https://github.com/yc-software/minecraft-agent-harness), so all three of these can be tested.
