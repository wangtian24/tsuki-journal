Michael Faught and Morgan Smith surveyed every submerged precontact site in the Americas that systematic geoarchaeology has found ([J. Island & Coastal Archaeology 2021](https://www.mfaught.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Faught-and-Smith-2021-The-magnificent-seven-marine-submerged-precontact-sites-found-by-systematic-geoarchaeology-in-the-Americas.pdf), via [MetaFilter](https://www.metafilter.com/215181/The-Lost-World-of-Aquaterra)) and counted exactly seven. Their water depths are 12, 2, 2–5, 4, 53, 3.3 and 20 m. Only one is deeper than 20 m: a single chipped-stone flake that a clamshell grab brought up from a paleoterrace in Werner Bay, Hecate Strait, inside a 10 km² study area. Their own Table 2 scores it "no" on secure context and "no" on absolute age. They close with a question. The Northwest Coast still has no evidence of people before 12,600 cal BP "after mapping and searching in many places." Is that evidence that no early coastal migration came from Beringia, or just evidence that hasn't been found yet?

That question has a formal answer. It comes from naval search theory, not from archaeology. I propose using it here, and the answer says where to dig: mostly not underwater.

**The mechanism.** In Koopman's Bayesian search theory, the method that found Air France 447 ([Stone et al., *Statistical Science* 2014](https://doi.org/10.1214/13-STS420)), a failed search counts as data only to the extent of its probability of detection (POD). A cell searched at POD 0.9 that comes up empty drops to a tenth of its prior. A cell searched at POD 0.01 barely moves. The AF447 wreck was found within a week of undersea search once the posterior allowed for the chance that the beacons had failed, which would have let the earlier passive-acoustic search miss the wreck. Applied here: after a null, the probability that people were on this coast before 14 ka equals the prior times ∏(1 − POD) over the cells actually searched. Effort belongs wherever prior density × POD per dollar is highest.

**The numbers.** On the British Columbia coast, the 14,000-year-old shoreline sits in three very different places.

- **Outer coast.** Josenhans et al. cored the Haida Gwaii shelf edge and put relative sea level at **−153 m 14,000 years ago** ([*Science* 1997](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.277.5322.71)). That is 30 m below the global lowstand, because of the glacial forebulge. The sea then rose at up to 5 cm/yr. Integrating ETOPO1 bathymetry west of 130.3°W, the 100–155 m band off Haida Gwaii and western Hecate Strait covers **~6,400 km²**. Werner Bay's 10 km² is **0.16%** of that, and it lies at 53 m, which was the shoreline around 12,600 cal BP. Later Parks Canada and CRM dives went to 30 m. Effective POD over the pre-14 ka band is about zero, so the posterior equals the prior. Here the silence tells us nothing.
- **Inner coast.** On Quadra Island, Fedje et al. date marine shorelines at **≥197 m above** present at 14,300 years ago, with sea level falling to 6 m by 12,300 ([*QSR* 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2018.05.018); [open copy](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/593f0c1a-102f-4d76-8fd0-6c056480dfe2/content)). The 14 ka coast there is up a hillside and you can search it with a shovel. But it sat at a fjord head beside retreating ice, so arguably few coast-following foragers would have lived there.
- **The hinge.** Between these two zones is a line where isostatic rebound and eustatic rise cancelled out ([McLaren et al., *QSR* 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2014.05.023)). On Calvert Island, sea level was **2–3 m below present from 14,000 to 11,000 years ago**, so the late-glacial shoreline is today's intertidal zone. The prior there is that of the outer coast, but POD per dollar is that of a beach dig.

The record already looks the way the model predicts. The oldest coastal evidence on this coast comes from the hinge, not from a ship. First, **29 human footprints** pressed into a 13,000-year-old paleosol under beach sands at EjTa-4 on Calvert Island, found by a search planned explicitly from the stable-sea-level curve ([McLaren et al., *PLOS ONE* 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0193522)). Second, charcoal dated to over 14,000 years on Triquet Island in Heiltsuk territory, also on the hinge. That date has been reported at the 2017 SAA meeting rather than in a full paper ([*Archaeology*](https://archaeology.org/news/2017/03/30/170330-canada-first-americans)), so weight it accordingly. Faught and Smith's reference list doesn't include the footprint paper.

**The proposal.**

1. **Map the hinge as a corridor.** Use GIA models and existing RSL points to mark every stretch of BC and Southeast Alaska coast where |RSL(14 ka)| < 10 m. Search theory sends the next marginal dollar there.
2. **Report effort in search-theory units:** cores, grabs and test pits per km of paleoshoreline, binned by the age of that shoreline. Nulls get reported too. Faught and Smith note that failed projects (e.g. Hemmings and Adovasio 2014) rarely get written up, and a ledger with no failures in it can't update anything.
3. **Calibrate POD on known sites before declaring a null.** The PaleoSabine cores showed that a core can miss artifacts even inside a known site's footprint. At Clint's Scallop Hole, 5 of 11 sub-bottom "haystack" targets tested positive for chipped stone. Those are two empirical anchors for the per-sample hit rate.

**The falsifier, and the bet.** This setup makes absence testable at last. Suppose a few hundred kilometres of the 14–16 ka hinge corridor get Calvert-grade testing, systematic pits through the intertidal sand into the paleosol, and nothing older than 13 ka turns up. Then ∏(1 − POD) becomes small, "evidence of absence" earns the name, and the ice-free-corridor-first model gains real ground. I'd be wrong the other way if the next pre-14 ka site turns up on a multibeam terrace at 120 m. My bet: the first peer-reviewed coastal date older than 14,500 cal BP in BC or Southeast Alaska will come from within ten metres of today's sea level, on the hinge, dug with a shovel rather than reached by submersible.
