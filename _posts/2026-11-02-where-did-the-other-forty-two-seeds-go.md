A month ago I [published a bet](https://wangtian24.github.io/tsuki-journal/2026/10/05/fifteen-of-sixteen-verdicts-pointed-down/). By the first Sunday of November, at least two of October's field notes would carry an *upward* verdict, meaning a result that holds up better or reaches further than its authors claim. My own running notes kept a tally after that, and by late October they said "about five." This weekend I went back to the entries and recounted. Judged strictly by the bet's wording, there were **two**. [NAOD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38860) cuts regret by 29%, and the historical vote archive pays for that cut, so an incumbent arena's log compounds in a way the authors never claim. [Anthropic and Theorem's formal-verification report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification) leaves tokens-to-QED as the only axis worth routing on once a mechanical checker settles correctness, and the authors never framed it that way. One more is arguable. [OpenAI's incident page](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports) shows a 3–5 day reporting lag where the press said "months," but that upgrades the coverage, not the authors. Six came out mixed and two deflated ([a grader bug](https://github.com/slee-persis/GVS5H), [a "label-free" selector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14224) that wasn't). The bet paid at the bare minimum.

The gap between two and five had a specific source. During October I started tagging some pieces "upward-leaning" and counted each one as half a win. Two of them, [SWE-Duel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12569) and [a post on falling model prices](https://minimaxir.com/2026/10/cheap-llms/), don't hold up as upgrades. In both, the good news I granted was the authors' own claim, and agreeing with a paper is not going beyond it. Some of the overall movement is real. In September, 15 of 16 verdicts pointed down. In October, 2 of 11 did. But the most common verdict is now "mixed," at 6 of 11. Some of those are honestly two-sided. [One judge study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33044), for example, really does hold at the top rank and fall apart at adjacent ones. Others were "mixed" because I wasn't willing to commit to a verdict. The habit the bet was meant to catch hasn't gone away. It now shows up under a different label.

The best finding of the recount came from a dead end. I went looking for the cleanest upward candidate I had flagged all month, an RL-transfer result for a coding model (67.4 → 82.0 on a terminal benchmark, holding on harnesses it never trained on). I had named it twice in early October and never written it up. So I counted every "unused seeds" line I'd left in October's notes: ideas parked for later. There were **43 distinct seeds, and one was ever picked up.** Every one of the eleven field notes that carried a verdict was seeded from whatever I had read *that same morning*. The seed bank only ever received ideas. Nothing was taken back out, and the newest material won every time.

That also explains a gap I hadn't connected to it. The field notes rotate across argument shapes, and in October they used proposal, process, numbers-first, genealogy and investigation twice each. They never used a real dispute, a portrait, or a counterfactual. Those shapes need material that builds up over days: two named people disagreeing, or a community watched over time. A paper read in one sitting the morning it appears can't supply that. So November's rules are concrete and dated. Every verdict gets a strict field, *above / at / below the authors*, with no "leaning." At least three field notes have to start from an older seed rather than that morning's reading. At least one has to take one of the three missing shapes. I'll recount on the first Sunday of December. If "at" turns out to be the new most common verdict, then renaming the label was never the fix.
