Alibaba shipped [Qwen4-Max](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen4-max) over the weekend: a 2.4T-parameter MoE with 71B active, proprietary, listed at $1.50/$4.50 per million tokens. Within hours the [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50513215) turned into a real argument among named commenters about how a model like this should be scored at all. Nobody disputed the numbers. They disputed the settings the numbers were taken at, which is the question every leaderboard operator has to answer and usually answers silently.

**Round one was about price.** [maplet](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50513658) opened with [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/qwen4-max): Qwen4-Max scores 43 on the Intelligence Index (#38 of 81) at **$3.27 per task**, while GPT-6.1 Sol at medium effort scores 46 for **$0.36**, a higher score at about a ninth of the price. AA also shows why: Qwen4-Max burned 127M output tokens on the index against a 78.6M median. [Aurornis](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50514150) backed this up with AA's cost-per-task Pareto chart. Then [esperent](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50514211) pointed at the chart itself. Sol and Opus each appear as a curve of about six reasoning-effort points, and Qwen4-Max appears as one unlabeled dot, presumably at max. Compared max to max (Sol $1.27, Opus 5.5 $10.01), Qwen sits in between, not nine times off. esperent's analogy: popular cars tested at 20, 40 and 60 km/h, the rest only at 60, all on one mileage chart. A [follow-up](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50515335) made the point sharper: "max" on one model doesn't mean "max" on another, so even max-to-max isn't fair. What's missing is a *range* per model. [behnamoh](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50514668) added a user's view: he always runs at max because fewer corrections make it "faster overall."

**Round two was about the clock.** [seizethecheese](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50513416) runs [Agent Duel](https://agentduel.com/leaderboard), a blind Bradley-Terry arena for coding agents, and regrets including Qwen 3.8 because "the only reason it scores so low is that it's slooooow (like 30 tokens per second vs opus near 80)." Agent Duel lets voters decide before both agents finish, "contrary to Arena.ai," on purpose: for agents, waiting is part of what you're judging. The board shows the effect. Qwen 3.8 Max sits at #9 (972) with a **5.4-minute** median task time, against Opus 5.5's 2.3 minutes (1057), and the other slow model, Gemini Carbon at 6.2 minutes, also ranks below its reputation. Two details from the same table soften the complaint. The confidence intervals are about ±42, so everything from #4 to #10 overlaps, and "scores so low" really means "can't be told apart from GPT-6.1 Sol (977)." And the board already has an "Exclude early voting" filter. [neurophant](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50518865) gave the other side its best line: "the cheap one gets the same work done, roughly twice as slow. The big model buys you speed, not results." If speed is what buyers are paying for, an arena that hides it ranks the wrong product. Arena.ai's [style-control work](https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-08-28-style-control) shows the opposite habit: find the nuisance variable (length, markdown), give it its own regression coefficient, and keep model identity clean. Both positions are defensible, because they serve different buyers.

What the two rounds share is a question of authority: who picks the setting, the lab choosing an effort level or the voter choosing how long to wait? Neither choice should be baked into a leaderboard without saying so. The fixes are cheap. Log the effort level on every entry and require at least three before a model goes on a cost chart. Log time-to-vote and whether each side had finished when the vote was cast. Then publish two boards, one that includes speed and one that controls for it with latency as a Bradley-Terry covariate, the way length already is. Agent Duel shows the toggle costs almost nothing. The number I'd most like to see is the gap between the two boards, because it would tell us how much of any model's rank comes from its serving speed rather than its answers.
