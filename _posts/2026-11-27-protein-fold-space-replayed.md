Stephen Jay Gould's thought experiment was to rewind life to the Burgess Shale and play the tape again. Protein structure lets you run a sharper version, because one layer of that tape has now been mapped from both sides: what nature built, and what could have been built. The natural catalogue is small and slowing down. A new review by Sahakyan and Koonin ([arXiv:2611.33104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33104)) counts "slightly over 1,000" characterized folds against tens of thousands of sequence families, and the Encyclopedia of Domains ([Lau et al., *Science* 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq4946)) cut 214 million AlphaFold models into about 365 million domains and still found fewer than 10,000 distinct folds. The possible catalogue comes from Koga's lab ([Minami et al., *NSMB* 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01029-0)). An open n-stranded β-sheet with helical connections has n!·2^(n−2) topologies, from 12 at three strands to 2.58 million at eight. They wrote down the rules natural sheets obey (right-handed β-α-β crossovers, short jumps, which side loops sit on), sorted every topology into "frustration-free" or "frustrated", and counted how much of the allowed space nature actually uses:

| Strands | Frustration-free topologies | Seen in nature | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 10 | 10 | 100% |
| 4 | 43 (of 96) | 35 | 81% |
| 5–8 | >10,000 unobserved, plus the observed | a few hundred at most | under ~6% |

The last row is my bound, not theirs: αβ folds are a bit over half of the ~1,000-fold catalogue, so observed five-to-eight-strand folds can't exceed ~600, against a frustration-free space above 10,600. The gaps are not physics. Minami et al. designed all eight unobserved or barely observed four-strand topologies, including a knotted parallel 3-1-4-2 sheet "long considered to be impossible", and all eight folded close to the design model, as stably as designs based on natural folds. So run the replay. A second biosphere sampling the same allowed space would have no choice at three strands and would mostly rebuild our 35 at four. At five to eight strands, two independent draws of ~600 from ~10,600 share only about 600²/10,600 ≈ 34 folds. That is a toy figure: real usage is skewed toward a few attractive topologies like the ferredoxin-like fold, which raises the overlap. But even a large-fold catalogue sharing a third with ours would be mostly foreign. The boundary sits between four strands and five.

Two experiments say a replay would reach new folds easily. PFES ([Sahakyan et al., *PNAS* 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2509015122)) evolves random sequences under selection for foldability; only 82 of its 200 evolved proteins had natural structural analogues, and known and novel folds took about the same number of mutations (407 against 389). Nature runs the same experiment in phage anti-CRISPR proteins, young genes under arms-race pressure: of 102 families searched, 75 matched no known structure. The weighty dissent is [Zhang, Skolnick and colleagues (*PNAS* 2006)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0509379103), who found that random compact chains all had PDB analogues and called the fold universe "highly likely complete". That doesn't refute the replay so much as set its resolution. At the coarse level of how secondary structures pack (TM-score around 0.4), the space is small and saturated, and a second biosphere would match ours. At the level of strand order and connection handedness, which is what Minami counts, it is nowhere near saturated. Koonin himself concedes that a fold is "to some extent, a subjective category". The PFES number is also softer than it looks, since its fitness oracle is a structure predictor trained on natural proteins; the Minami table rests on NMR structures, which is why it carries the argument.

What it changes is how to read the big ancient folds. The Rossmann, the P-loop NTPase and the TIM barrel are usually treated as near-inevitable answers to chemistry. On this evidence they look like frozen accidents of size: whatever happened to be found first among thousands of equally good options, then reused for four billion years. A second origin would almost certainly have nucleotide-binding domains, but probably not the Rossmann. The bet, with its falsifier: when random-sequence or in vitro evolution runs reach 100+ residues and are checked experimentally, the novel-fold fraction should rise with chain length, clearly above the ~59% PFES saw at ~56 residues. If it stays flat or falls, the four-strand boundary is an artifact of Minami's rules, and the "nature has exhausted fold space" camp gets a real point back.
