Forty-three arcseconds per century. That's the whole empirical anchor: Mercury's perihelion precesses a little faster than Newton's gravity, plus the pull of Venus and Jupiter, can account for. Mercury takes 88 days to orbit, so 43″/century works out to 0.103 arcseconds of extra swing *per orbit* — a fractional precession of 8.0×10⁻⁸. That one number, known since Le Verrier in 1859, is the sole piece of data [David Wallace's November 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20210) needs to fix the one free constant in a theory built from nothing but Newtonian mechanics and bookkeeping.

The build runs in four stages, and it breaks — the same way — twice. Stage one takes Newtonian gravity and reinterprets it, in the spirit of Einstein's 1907 equivalence principle, as a *localized* inertial force: instead of a universal field pulling on everything, gravity is what you get when "unaccelerated" is allowed to vary from place to place. Demanding the field equations look the same in any patch of empty space, sourced only by mass, linear, momentum-conserving, pins down the inverse-square law essentially uniquely — nothing new yet, this is the textbook story. Stage two localizes rotation as well: Newtonian spacetime has a global standard of rotation that was never made local, so a rotating frame is made to produce a Coriolis field exactly as a current produces a magnetic field, with a new constant K in a gravitational Ampère's law. Taking the divergence of that law and combining it with mass conservation forces K∂ₜρ = 0 for *all* ρ — consistent only if K = 0. Gravitomagnetism, sourced by moving matter, is simply incompatible with mass conservation, and the usual patch (keep the field, forbid matter from sourcing it) is a dead end.

Stage three localizes further, to the full conformal group of rotations, translations and scale. A parallel divergence trick forces the dimensionless coupling β = 4 — but a second mismatch opens immediately. Writing the mass source as ρ* = Ωᵠρ, where Ω is the local scale factor, full momentum conservation requires q = 4; conformal symmetry itself requires q = 3. Off by exactly one, and you can satisfy either requirement, not both. The repair, stage four, is to localize one more thing: temporal scale, letting the relativity principle itself vary locally instead of assuming Galilean time is absolute. Redo the bookkeeping with that extra field in play and q = 2n − m = 5 satisfies both constraints at once. What emerges is unique up to one velocity constant, and it is — provably, not by design — the first post-Newtonian approximation to general relativity. Feed in Mercury's 8.0×10⁻⁸ and its orbital speed of 4.8×10⁴ m/s, and the fitted constant comes out at 3.00×10⁸ m/s, with no electromagnetism assumed anywhere in the derivation.

The same construction predicts that light, a fast test particle in the limit v→c, is deflected by the sun by *twice* the amount a naive application of the equivalence principle alone would give, because a velocity-dependent force tied to localized scale adds an equal second deflection on top of the ordinary gravitational one. That doubling isn't a new fact about light; it's stage three's unresolved mismatch in a different guise, with the equivalence-principle reasoning supplying half the bend and localized scale supplying the other half. History ran the same script by accident: Einstein's 1911 equivalence-principle estimate gave 0.87 arcseconds, exactly half of the 1.75 arcseconds his 1915 field equations predicted and Eddington's 1919 eclipse expedition measured, because 1911-Einstein had done stages one and two but not yet three. (Paul Gerber had gotten Mercury's 43″ right in 1898 from a retarded-potential guess with no equivalence principle at all; Einstein's verdict on Gerber's derivation was that it was "wrong through and through" — right number, no mechanism.)

So the lesson isn't that gravity is secretly electromagnetism, or that Newton and Einstein were closer than textbooks admit — those are the comfortable readings a paper like this invites. The sharper fact is structural: every time one more piece of inertial structure gets localized, momentum conservation and the new symmetry disagree by a specific, computable integer offset, and the offset is exactly what the next localization absorbs. The light-bending factor of two that textbooks present as a free-standing relativistic surprise is the same arithmetic defect that already broke gravitomagnetism-by-itself one stage earlier, showing up again because the construction hadn't yet paid off its second debt. Bet: any "intuitive derivation" of general relativity's light-bending factor that doesn't separately localize scale will reproduce only the equivalence-principle half, and will quietly assume the other half as given.
