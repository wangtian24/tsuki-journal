Last week the Faiss team at Meta FAIR released **e-means** ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09501) · [site](https://e-means.github.io/) · [code](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss/tree/main/contrib/e_means)). It is mini-batch k-means in which each cluster's sum and count decay as an exponential moving average, with a cosine-annealed step and batch size and step taken from fitted scaling laws. The main claim is speed, and it is well supported: on 11 VIBE embedding sets it matches Lloyd's 25-epoch test error in 1.6–2.4 epochs. The site makes a second claim as well, that e-means "better escapes local minima." It backs this with 2-D toys, the clearest being five unit-variance blobs on a line with a light middle blob. There Lloyd "stays stuck on 46% of starts, while e-means stays stuck on 0%," and the explanation offered is that noisy mini-batches explore while Lloyd's monotone descent exploits. I wanted to know what was being escaped, so I rebuilt that toy: weights 1 : 1 : 0.3 : 1 : 1, n = 20,000, k = 5, random data-point initialization, sklearn's Lloyd capped at 25 iterations as on the site, and the shipped `NumpyEmeans` at its defaults. "Stuck" means more than 1% above the optimum. At 3σ, 4σ and 5σ spacing, e-means was stuck on 0% of 100 seeds and Lloyd on 21–49%. At 6σ both were stuck on 53%, and giving e-means five times as many epochs changed nothing. If noise were doing the escaping, the advantage should fade gradually as the barriers grow. It doesn't fade; it falls off a cliff. Noise also can't move centroids far enough: at α ≈ 0.3 with about 25 points per cluster per batch, the EMA moves a centroid about 0.08σ per step, nowhere near enough to cross a 5σ gap.

In one dimension, the infinite-data k-means objective and its Hessian have closed forms in Gaussian CDFs, so I could follow the exact population Lloyd map and classify every fixed point it reached from 400 random starts. Up to a spacing of 5.25σ, every start reaches the global optimum, so there is no spurious minimum to escape. At 5.5σ one appears. It has 1.57× the optimal loss and a positive-definite Hessian, and it catches 189 of the 400 starts. Its shape is the one [Qian, Zhang & Chen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06694) prove is essentially the only kind of spurious k-means minimum under separation: two centroids share one blob, and one centroid straddles two. Following it down in spacing and bisecting puts its birth at **s\* = 5.416σ**, a saddle-node bifurcation. Just below a saddle-node the minimum is gone, but it leaves a ghost: a narrow channel where the descent map nearly stalls. The textbook signature is a passage time that grows like 1/√(s\* − s). From a fixed start, exact Lloyd needs 25 iterations to get within 1% of the optimum at s = 5.0, 45 at 5.3, and 340 at 5.414. The square-root law predicts a ratio of 7.8 between the last two; I measured 7.5. So the 25-iteration cap stops Lloyd inside the ghost. With max_iter = 100, its stuck rate at 5σ drops from 49% to 3% with no noise at all, and it stays at 3% at 2,000 iterations. At 6σ, past the bifurcation, it stays at 53% at every budget, the same as e-means.

What e-means brings to the ghost is the number of updates. Five epochs at batch 128 is 785 centroid updates, against Lloyd's 25. Holding s = 5 and varying only the batch size, the escape rate follows the update count:

| batch | centroid updates | stuck |
|---|---|---|
| 128 | 785 | 0% |
| 1,024 | 100 | 14% |
| 5,000 | 20 | 56% |
| 20,000 (α = 1, i.e. Lloyd) | 5 | 84% |

The other toys mostly agree once Lloyd runs to convergence. On a square with a heavy centre, Lloyd's 3% falls to 0%, matching e-means. On two blobs of unequal size it is a tie (40.0% vs 40.5%). The exception is a 3×3 grid with heavy corners, where e-means is stuck on 29% and converged Lloyd on 46%, so there is some real basin-hopping in two dimensions, though far less than the headline suggests. (Caveat: these toys are rebuilt from the site's descriptions, not the authors' data, and none of this touches the paper's high-dimensional speed results.)

So in these toys, the speed claim and the escape claim turn out to be the same claim. E-means makes hundreds of cheap updates where Lloyd makes dozens of expensive ones, and that gets it through slow channels within a fixed epoch budget. That is worth having, but it does not protect against true spurious minima. [Jin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.00978) showed such minima exist even for arbitrarily well-separated mixtures and catch random initialization with high probability. Against those, seeding is still the tool: [k-means++](https://theory.stanford.edu/~sergei/papers/kMeansPP-soda.pdf) cut the 6σ stuck rate from 53% to about 9% for both algorithms. My bet: on any benchmark that runs Lloyd to convergence instead of a fixed 25 epochs, e-means' advantage in final *loss* will shrink to about the size of the grid residual, while its wall-clock advantage stays intact. A detail in [the shipped code](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss/blob/main/contrib/e_means/base.py) points the same way: it turns dead-cluster revival off for d ≤ 2, because on 2-D data revival *raised* held-out error by 7–10%. In two dimensions, the authors' own tuning found that extra stirring hurts.
